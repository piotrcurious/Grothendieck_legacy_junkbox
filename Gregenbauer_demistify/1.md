3. Deriving the Asymptotic Terms Geometrically
When evaluating this orbital integral in the limit n \to \infty using the Duistermaat-Heckman theorem or the stationary phase approximation, the exact analytical features of the asymptotic formula emerge structurally:
 * The Oscillatory Cosine Factor \cos\left((n+\lambda)\theta - \phi\right):
   The stationary points of the action S(\xi, \theta) on the orbit \mathcal{O}_n correspond to the critical geodesics connecting the two H-fixed points. There are exactly two critical points on the orbit, corresponding to clockwise and counterclockwise rotations by \theta. Evaluating the action S at these two points yields e^{+i n \theta} and e^{-i n \theta}, which combine to give the cosine wave.
 * The Amplitude Factor (\sin\theta)^{-\lambda} (Van Vleck-Morette Determinant):
   The factor (\sin\theta)^{-\lambda} = (\sin\theta)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}} is the inverse square root of the Hessian determinant of the action at the critical points. Geometrically, this determinant represents the Jacobian of the projection map from the coadjoint orbit \mathcal{O}_n down to the double coset space H \backslash G / H. Near \theta = \pi/2, the orbit projects cleanly, but as \theta \to 0, the fibers degenerate, causing the amplitude to concentrate.
 * The Quantization Correction (n + \lambda):
   The shift n \to n + \lambda (where \lambda = \frac{d-2}{2} = \rho) is the half-sum of positive roots (\rho-shift) in Lie theory. It corresponds to the Maslov index correction in semiclassical mechanics and canonical quantization.
