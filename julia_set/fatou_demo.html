1 · Orthogonality → recurrence → map

For a positive measure on the real line, monic orthogonal polynomials satisfy a three-term recurrence

Qₙ₊₁(z) = (z − bₙ)Qₙ(z) − aₙ²Qₙ₋₁(z)

The measure determines the recurrence coefficients. Here the chosen finite-degree polynomial is then used as a dynamical map, f(z)=Qd(z)+c. The recurrence determines the map; iteration determines its Fatou and Julia structure.

2 · OPUC / CMV

For orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle, Verblunsky coefficients encode the measure and a unitary CMV matrix replaces the real-line Jacobi matrix. A future module could use these coefficients to generate a polynomial map or study the CMV spectrum alongside the dynamical plane.

Not simulated by this current renderer.

3 · Isospectral deformation / Toda

Toda evolution changes Jacobi recurrence coefficients while preserving the spectrum of the associated Jacobi operator. Rebuilding Qd along that flow would let the explorer ask whether spectral invariants constrain, or fail to constrain, the resulting Fatou geometry.

This is a proposed extension, not a time-integrated Toda solver.

4 · Beyond polynomial maps

Extensions can include custom recurrence coefficients, non-orthogonal polynomials, compositions of maps, and rational maps. Rational maps add poles and can have Fatou components (such as Herman rings) that cannot occur for polynomial maps.

The dynamical partition

For a polynomial map of degree at least two, the filled Julia set K(f) contains points with bounded forward orbit; the Julia set is its boundary, J(f)=∂K(f); the Fatou set is the region where the iterates form a normal family. Its connected components are Fatou components.

The basin of infinity is always a Fatou component. Other Fatou components can be attracting or parabolic basins, or rotation domains such as Siegel disks.

What the colours mean

Escape palette: points numerically escaping to infinity. Cycle colours: points detected as converging to attracting cycles of period 1–8. Dark region: bounded within the iteration budget, but not classified by this simple cycle detector.

It does not detect every Fatou component: Siegel disks, parabolic behaviour, long transients and boundary points may remain unclassified. Two distinct cycles can still receive similar colours. Finite computation cannot certify membership in the Fatou set.

Important distinction

Orthogonality belongs to Qd as a polynomial in a real variable with respect to a measure. It does not itself imply a particular Julia set. Once Qd is iterated over ℂ, the recurrence coefficients and c control the dynamics together. A finite polynomial taken from an orthogonal sequence is not the same thing as iterating the whole sequence index n.

Numerical caveats

Escape and cycle detection use finite tolerances and a finite iteration budget. The potential image is a visual estimate, not a certified Green function or capacity calculation. Blue dots estimate zeros of Qd; rose crosses estimate critical points Q′d(z)=0. These are different objects. Toggle critical-orbit traces to see whether critical dynamics appear to escape or approach an attracting cycle; this finite-time test is suggestive, not a proof of the full component structure.
